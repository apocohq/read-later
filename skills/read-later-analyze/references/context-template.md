# Reader context

_Written by the host agent. Sent to an ephemeral evaluator together with each article, so keep it to what evaluation needs: no contact details, no secrets, no third-party personal data. About one page. Refresh it when the reader's focus shifts._

## Who the reader is

Role, field, seniority, the kind of decisions they make. Two or three sentences.

## What they are working on now

The 3 to 6 live projects or questions, one line each. This drives `relevance`.

## What they already know well

Areas where an introductory piece adds nothing. This drives `novelty` and `redundancy`.

## What they want more of

Kinds of material they consistently find valuable: first-hand engineering accounts, contrarian takes with evidence, primary sources, whatever applies.

## What they want less of

Things to score down: vendor content, hype, listicles, topics they have deliberately parked.

## Recently read or saved

Optional. Titles or themes from the last few weeks, so near-duplicates score high on `redundancy`.
